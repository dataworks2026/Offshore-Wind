### [w1-maint:baseline_cadence]
DNVGL-ST-0376 Clause 16.2 prescribes offshore rotor blades receive external visual inspections at least every 12 months, with tactile close-up examinations of the leading edge and bondlines every 6 months where significant wave height exceeds 2 m or salt spray is heavy. For onshore fleets, the standard allows annual close inspection but requires an additional interim drone or binocular survey following any severe weather event exceeding design reference turbulence.

### [w1-maint:condition_based_triggers]
EPRI’s 2022 maintenance quantification study correlates leading-edge recession >0.3 mm or structural crack indications >50 mm with a “Priority 1” work order, requiring planning for repair within 60 days and scheduling of NDT within 7 days of detection. The same study recommends that once two consecutive inspections show erosion growth >0.1 mm/month, interim monitoring should shift to 45-day cadence until LEP or repair is applied.

### [w1-maint:post_repair_followup]
BSEE TAP-627AA outlines that structural repairs completed offshore must be followed by verification inspections at 1, 3, and 6 months, including ultrasonic or thermographic checks of scarf joints and adhesive bonds. DNVGL-ST-0376 reinforces this by mandating documentation of residual strength demonstrations (proof load or strain monitoring) before returning turbines to full power, and adding the repaired location to a heightened observation list for the remainder of the operating year.
